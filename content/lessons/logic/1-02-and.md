"The number is even **and** greater than 10." A statement joined by *and* claims two things at once. Logicians write `A ∧ B`.

As with if-then, there are two skills: **proving** an and, and **using** one you have.

## Proving an and

To prove "A and B" you prove A, and you prove B. There is nothing more to it. The move **constructor** says so to Lean: it replaces the goal `A ∧ B` by two goals, first A and then B.

The board shows the goal you are on, and the other one waiting under "Then one more goal". Lean labels the two `left` and `right`.

```exercise
id: prove-both
```

## Using an and

If you know "A and B", you know A, and you know B. The move **obtain** takes the fact apart: one card becomes two.

Look at the line it writes: `obtain ⟨hP, hQ⟩ := h`. Read it as "from h, obtain hP and hQ". The angle brackets `⟨ ⟩` hold the names for the pieces, in order. The board picks the names; when you type, you pick them.

```exercise
id: take-apart
```

## Both at once

To turn "P and Q" into "Q and P" you need both skills: take the fact apart, then build the goal from the pieces.

```exercise
id: swap
```

The order of your moves was up to you. You could split the goal first and take the fact apart afterwards. Both are proofs. One caution: a fact taken apart after the goal is split is only taken apart for the goal you are on, so it is usually tidier to take facts apart first.

## What you used

- `constructor` to prove `A ∧ B`: two goals, A then B.
- `obtain ⟨hA, hB⟩ := h` to use `h : A ∧ B`: two facts, `hA : A` and `hB : B`.
